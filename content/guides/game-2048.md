[2048](/tools/game-2048/) is a four-by-four sliding-tile puzzle. Equal numbered tiles that meet merge into one larger tile; each *successful* slide adds a new `2` or `4`. Reach `2048` to see a win message, but you can continue playing.

## Make your first move

Open the game: **Score** starts at `0` and two numbered tiles appear on the board. Press an arrow key (or `W`, `A`, `S`, `D`, or swipe on touch) to slide the tiles. The board changes only if a tile can move or merge. If two `2` tiles merge, they form `4` and **Score** increases by `4`; the starting positions are random, so the first move may be different for you. **Undo** restores the board and score from the last successful move. **New game** resets the board and score.

**Best** is saved in this browser's local storage, even when you start a new game. Clearing site data removes it. Nothing is uploaded; this is not a shared leaderboard.

## Where your input goes

Processing runs in your browser. The tool does not upload your input to ilham.dev.

## Questions you might have

### How does merging work?

A move slides every tile as far as it will go in that direction. Two tiles of the same value that meet merge into one tile of double the value, and a tile that has already merged once in a move cannot merge again. That single-merge rule is why sliding left can pair the first two tiles but leave the third of a triple alone.

### Where is my best score kept?

In this browser's local storage, under a key that belongs to this page. It survives reloads and never leaves your device. Clearing site data resets it.

### What does undo do?

It steps back exactly one move, including the score you gained and the random tile that appeared. There is only one level of undo, so use it to recover from a mis-swipe rather than to rewind a whole game.

### Why did the game end when I still had empty squares?

This implementation ends the round only when **no empty cell remains and no adjacent pair can merge**. If there is an empty cell, another move is still possible somewhere—even if the direction you just pressed did nothing.

### Is there a strategy?

Most strong players keep their largest tile in one corner and build a descending chain along two edges, never moving the corner tile away. Deciding early which corner that is matters more than any single merge.
